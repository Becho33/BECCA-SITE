Frequently asked questions

Clear answers about consultations, assessments, costs, waiting times and the scope of our work.

Do you take on parent-commissioned work?+

Yes, although SEP prioritises school-commissioned work, such as acting as a link EP working directly with schools. This supports equity of access for children and young people and helps ensure that schools are committed to collaborative work that can make a positive difference.

Do you carry out virtual assessments?+

Our preference is to carry out face-to-face assessments wherever possible. Virtual assessment may be considered in exceptional circumstances, depending on the case.

How much do you charge?+

Costs depend on the service, what you hope to achieve through EP involvement and the level of support required. These points are discussed and agreed during an initial consultation. Schools can also view our service packages and pricing.

What does an initial consultation involve?+

Before casework begins, an initial consultation is held with the person making the referral, such as a family member or member of school staff. This provides information about the EP role; gathers an overview of the young person’s strengths and needs; explores what would be helpful; outlines and agrees possible EP involvement; explains costs; answers questions; and maps out the next steps.

How long is your waiting list?+

This depends on the service required. We can typically offer an initial consultation within one month, consultation-based work within two months and assessment work within three months. Please contact us for current availability and waiting times.

Do you diagnose dyslexia, dyscalculia, ADHD or autism?+

We do not typically provide a formal diagnosis. Our role focuses on understanding individual strengths and needs, exploring possible explanations for areas of concern and identifying personalised support. Autism, ADHD and dyspraxia diagnoses are medical in nature and must be assessed and confirmed by an appropriate medical practitioner or multidisciplinary team.

Do you assess for EHCPs, EHCP appeals or tribunals?+

We do not name or recommend specific provisions or types of provision, prepare reports specifically for SEND tribunals, or undertake expert or professional witness work. We are a non-statutory service independent of local authorities.

How can I find an EP who works as an expert witness?+

Please consult the British Psychological Society, the Association of Child Psychologists in Private Practice or the HCPC register. A legal representative may also be able to direct you to a suitable professional.

Can you assess for exam access arrangements?+

Schools and SENCOs are typically responsible for assessing whether a student meets the criteria and for applying to the Joint Council for Qualifications (JCQ). Decisions depend on evidence that an adjustment—such as extra time, a reader or a scribe—is the child’s typical way of working. A diagnosis is not required and cannot be used as evidence on its own.

Why might a standardised cognitive assessment be unsuitable for a child with a history of trauma?+

For children who have experienced developmental trauma, standardised scores can be misleading. Dysregulation may affect access to cognitive skills, meaning low scores could be incorrectly understood as skills deficits. If such assessments are used, their results require careful interpretation through a trauma-informed formulation.

What are dynamic cognitive assessments?+

Where cognitive assessment is appropriate, we typically use dynamic approaches. These explore cognitive strengths and needs alongside emotional factors that can influence learning, such as anxiety and motivation. They also help identify strategies that support progress.

Do you offer EP shadowing opportunities?+

We are happy to speak with aspiring EPs and answer questions, but usually cannot offer shadowing because of confidentiality, GDPR and data-protection requirements. Shadowing is not required to become an EP; applicants need to demonstrate that they have actively learned about the role. Information about training is available from the Association of Educational Psychologists and relevant universities.
